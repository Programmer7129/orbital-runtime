Radiation corrupts computation in orbit. No one protects the workload.

Commercial GPUs are moving to orbit at scale. Cosmic radiation flips bits inside the GPU while it computes — not in transmission — and no vendor-neutral software layer keeps a COTS GPU job alive through it. We build that layer, calibrated to flight-data upset rates and validated on datacenter silicon.

The problem

Computation, not communication

Optical and RF downlinks are a solved, error-corrected problem — NASA's TBIRD moved 4.8 TB error-free through the atmosphere at 200 Gbps. The unsolved problem is bits corrupted inside the GPU as it computes: single-event upsets, multi-bit upsets, functional interrupts (device crash/hang), and cumulative-dose degradation. ECC catches some single-bit memory flips. Nothing protects the training or inference job itself — corrupted weights, poisoned optimizer state, silent divergence, or a hung device.

Mechanism

Worse than intuition suggests

  • Format-level lethality. In fp32, for typical weights (|v|<1), flipping most exponent bits drives the value toward zero — harmless. Flipping bit 30 multiplies by ~2¹²⁸ → NaN cascade. Roughly 1 in 32 bits is lethal; a run absorbs ~100 upsets, then dies of one. Derived, not assumed — uniform-random injection; IEEE-754 decides which bit kills.
  • Silent corruption, not just crashes. Most failures announce themselves (NaN); a fraction finish with a measurably worse model and zero alarms — the case no downlink protocol or crash-monitor catches.
  • ECC shifts the threat, doesn't remove it. On ECC-protected GPUs the crash rate exceeds the silent-corruption rate by 2.2–2.7× [NSREC'21]; 31.5% of HBM upsets are multi-bit and can evade single-bit ECC [MICRO'21]. The deployable (ECC-on) regime is dominated by the channels with no published rates for modern accelerators.
  • It grows on two curves. Each transistor shrink lowers critical charge → more upsets: H100 memory MTBE is 3.2× worse than A100 [NCSA, SC25]. Unit count in orbit rises yearly. Fragility × deployment, both monotonic.
Why now

The rad-hard alternative is a non-starter for AI

First datacenter-class GPU in orbit: Starcloud-1 (NVIDIA H100), Nov 2025. Google Project Suncatcher (TPU clusters, ~2027). A SpaceX orbital-AI constellation filing. The rad-hardened path everyone is escaping: the RAD750 (Perseverance rover, ~$200k/unit) is ~1998-class compute — about 10⁶× slower than an H100. Every operator flies commercial silicon and inherits the radiation problem. Rad-hardening costs a million-fold compute penalty; our runtime costs ~2%.

What we built

Inject → detect → recover, and measured

Device-agnostic (CPU/MPS/CUDA), deterministic: protected and unprotected runs face a bit-identical bombardment, so the comparison is a controlled experiment.

  • Calibrated injector. Time-varying Poisson process over resident bits, band 1e-9…1e-7 upsets/bit-day (NASA NEPP / CREME96 / flight-data anchored); the South Atlantic Anomaly modeled as a phase-gated multiplier normalized to redistribute — not manufacture — daily upsets, reproducing the 80–97% SAA share seen in flight; MBU clustering [MICRO'21]; SEFI calibrated to Suncatcher's measured 2×10⁻¹¹ cm²/chip.
  • Three-tier detection. Near-free guards (NaN / grad-norm / loss-spike); sampled ABFT checksums on GEMMs (Huang–Abraham lineage), sampling keyed to orbital position; an ECC/Xid watcher on real hardware counters.
  • Orbit-aware recovery. Asynchronous checkpoints cadenced to save before SAA entry; detect → restore → replay.

The honest edge

Disclosed limitations

The ECC-off single-bit regime is the calibratable proxy; the ECC-on residual channels (MBU-leak / SRAM-logic / SEFI) carry conditions caveats from neutron/HBM2 source data. No TID/aging term (out of scope for a rate model). The SAA is modeled as a fixed-phase transit per orbit (idealized). Headline numbers are fp32; bf16/fp16 differ. Two adversarial reviews — methodology and artifact-evaluation — were run against this work; findings are fixed or disclosed, both in-repo.

The open experiment

The data no competitor can shortcut

No published campaign irradiates a device during training and reports application-level outcome distributions — verified unclaimed against the single-event-effects literature (inference-under-beam exists; training does not). A proton campaign at the Crocker 67 MeV beamline — where Google qualified its TPUs — with a Jetson-class device would measure the beam-vs-simulation agreement factor for tensor-level fault models of AI training, and calibrate the ECC-on residual channels our model currently carries as literature-anchored assumptions.

Beam validation requires scarce beam time, radiation-effects expertise, and it expires each chip generation — a recurring moat, not a one-time hurdle.
Defensibility

Why not just NVIDIA

NVIDIA ships primitives (cuda-checkpoint), not vertical reliability products — and never built the fault-tolerant-training layer even for the vastly larger terrestrial market (that came from ByteDance, Meta, Google, academia). Data Center revenue is $75.2B/quarter [Q1 FY27]; orbital GPU volume through 2029 does not move that number. Their own documentation states products are "not designed, authorized, or warranted to be suitable for use in… space… equipment." When NVIDIA decides an ecosystem layer matters, they buy the leader — Mellanox, Bright Computing, Run:ai (~$700M, 2024). The plan is to be that leader, holding cross-chip beam data, fleet telemetry, and flight heritage.

Position

A narrow wedge into a large room

Resilience is a privileged wedge: it sits beneath every workload on every node and sees every fault — the position from which observability and security platforms expanded from a single feature. The surface ahead: fleet observability, health-aware scheduling, an assurance/insurance data business, cross-environment reach (LEO → cislunar → terrestrial edge/HPC), and a per-chip-generation re-characterization subscription.
